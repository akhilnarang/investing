What a SIF is

  • A SEBI category between mutual funds and PMS: fund houses may run long-short and other strategies mutual funds cannot, with a ₹10 lakh minimum per investor (per AMC) and daily or periodic liquidity depending on the strategy.
    • {% if t.strategies %}
  • Strategies here: {% for s, n in t.strategies %}{{ s }} {{ n }}{{ ', ' if not loop.last else '.' }}{% endfor %}
    • {% endif %}
  • Net exposure is what makes a long-short fund different from a mutual fund. A fund 80% long and 10% short is net 70% long: it will move roughly like 70% of an equity fund. A fund at 37% long and 35% short is close to market-neutral and its return is mostly the spread between what it bought and what it sold, plus the cash yield.
  • They are not benchmarked on this page. A hybrid long-short strategy against the Nifty 500 total return index would tell you nothing; when the first cohort passes three years a proper comparison can follow.

Where the numbers come from

  • AMFI's SIF NAV history: daily NAVs of every SIF from its first day. Launch dates and every return here are computed from them. SIFs are absent from AMFI's NAVAll and from mfapi.in.
  • AMFI's SIF register: the plans, their ISINs and strategy categories. The fund house is the one AMFI files the same ISIN issuer's ordinary schemes under.
  • Kuvera, for the {{ t.net.n }} SIFs it lists: the dated expense ratio, AUM, manager and the month-end portfolio from which long, short and net exposure are derived. The rest show blanks until Kuvera or the AMC publishes them.
  • Past returns only, and very short ones. Not investment advice.
